# Frozen Theory 3.3 constitutive qualification report

## Executive result

The complete eight-stage qualification pipeline now runs end to end:

1. every trained component is serialized to a portable JSON artifact;
2. constitutive targets come from an independent relativistic
   kinetic-theory quadrature rather than a neural teacher;
3. the phase edge is trained on held-out noisy trajectories and accompanied
   by grouped-bootstrap uncertainty;
4. dissipation is generated by a covariant positive-semidefinite mobility
   operator;
5. multi-step BPTT includes explicit event-location jump derivatives and is
   compared with ordinary, conservative, and randomized-smoothing estimates;
6. Carter residual authority is continued upward under explicit KKT,
   Legendre, thermodynamic, characteristic, switching, and Lyapunov margins;
7. only the qualified frozen artifact is exported to Slang and tested on
   Vulkan; and
8. all methods, measurements, failed candidates, and limitations are
   preserved here and in machine-readable form.

The final composed model passes. Its selected Carter residual energy scale is

\[
1.509870755152482\times10^{-8},
\]

about 51% above the conservative `1e-8` scale in the first experiment ladder.
The next larger bisection candidate fails the reserved characteristic margin,
so the continuation stops for a physical reason rather than an arbitrary
epoch limit.

The canonical artifacts are:

- [`theory33_frozen_variants.json`](theory33_frozen_variants.json), the
  replayable weights, phase model, configuration, data hashes, and training

## Scientific scope

This work establishes a reproducible integration and qualification result. It
does not establish a new measured equation of state.

The new data are *independently generated physics benchmarks*: they are
computed by a standalone Maxwell–Jüttner moment quadrature and a separate
stochastic trajectory process. They are not observational or laboratory
measurements. The generator imports neither the Tesseract simulator nor a
neural constitutive module, so the earlier circular “network learns a target
generated by the same response family” problem is removed.

The kinetic construction is motivated by:

- the relativistic perfect-gas/Synge equation of state, for which the exact
  specific enthalpy is expressed through relativistic Maxwell–Boltzmann
  moments; see Mignone, Plewa, and Bodo,
  [Equation of state in relativistic magnetohydrodynamics](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2966.2007.11858.x);
- the Anderson–Witting relaxation-time model,
  [A relativistic relaxation-time model for the Boltzmann equation](https://doi.org/10.1016/0031-8914(74)90355-3);
- positive transport and conservation concerns in relativistic
  relaxation-time closures; see Rocha, Denicol, and Noronha,
  [Novel Relaxation Time Approximation to the Relativistic Boltzmann Equation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07489);
- input-convex potential construction from Amos, Xu, and Kolter,
  [Input Convex Neural Networks](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/amos17b.html);
- and hybrid-event sensitivities summarized by Kong et al.,
  [Saltation Matrices: The Essential Tool for Linearizing Hybrid Dynamical Systems](https://doi.org/10.1109/JPROC.2024.3440211).

The implemented quadrature is a deliberately compact benchmark, not a
claim to reproduce every coefficient of those works.

## 2. Independent kinetic EOS and transport data

### Generator boundary

[`theory33_kinetic_data.py`](theory33_kinetic_data.py) is standalone. It does
not import `theory33_hybrid`, `theory33_experiments`, or any neural module.
Data generation is run separately:

```powershell
python .\generate_theory33_kinetic_data.py `
    --output-directory .\data
```

Keeping generation in a separate process also prevents the numerical
quadrature library from sharing state with PyTorch training.

### Maxwell–Jüttner quadrature

In units with particle rest mass and \(c\) equal to one,

\[
E(p)=\sqrt{1+p^2}.
\]

The radial equilibrium weight is

\[
w(p;T)\propto p^2\exp\left[-\frac{E(p)-1}{T}\right].
\]

Subtracting the rest-mass exponent changes only the normalization and prevents
underflow at the cold edge. A 128-point Gauss–Legendre rule on
\(p\in[0,24]\) computes:

\[
\langle E\rangle,\qquad
\frac{P}{n}=\left\langle\frac{p^2}{3E}\right\rangle,\qquad
\frac{h}{n}=\langle E\rangle+\frac{P}{n}.
\]

The sampled domain is:

| Variable | Domain |
|---|---:|
| Number density | `[0.06, 0.18]` |
| Temperature | `[0.08, 0.80]`, geometrically spaced |
| Carrier fraction | `[0.025, 0.090]` |
| Relative drift | `[0, 0.016]` |

The generator produces `2,772` EOS/transport rows.

### Independent transport Gram matrix

The two kinetic basis functions are

\[
\phi_0(p)=1,\qquad
\phi_1(p)=\frac{E(p)-\langle E\rangle}{T}.
\]

The reference mobility is the weighted Gram matrix

\[
L_{ij}
=
\frac{\tau n}{3T}
\left\langle
v^2\phi_i\phi_j
\right\rangle,\qquad
v^2=\frac{p^2}{E^2},
\]

with a small positive diagonal floor. It is symmetric
positive-definite by construction because it is an integral of outer
products. Density, temperature, and carrier fraction change the matrix; drift
does not enter the linear mobility itself.

The independent drift-energy target is

\[
E_{\rm drift}
=
L_{11}\left(\frac12y^2+0.08y^4\right),
\qquad y=\frac{r}{0.016}.
\]

This quartic target is generated before and independently of the convex
network architecture.

\[
r_c
=
0.006+\delta_{\rm trajectory}

## 4. Covariant positive-semidefinite mobility

### Learned species mobility

The context MLP produces three Cholesky parameters:

\[
C_\phi
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\operatorname{softplus}(a)+\epsilon & 0\\
b & \operatorname{softplus}(c)+\epsilon
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
L_\phi=C_\phi C_\phi^{\mathsf T}.
\]

Consequently, symmetry and positive semidefiniteness do not depend on sampled

### Covariant spacetime operator

Let \(u^A\) be the baryon four-velocity and

\[
P^{AB}=g^{AB}+u^Au^B
\]

the spatial projector. For two force covectors \(X_{jB}\), the flux is

\[
J_i^A=-L_{ij}P^{AB}X_{jB}.
\]

The entropy production is

\[
\dot S
=
-X_{iA}J_i^A
=
X_{iA}L_{ij}P^{AB}X_{jB}
\ge 0.
\]

The implementation derives the spatial force direction covariantly from the
relative carrier current and uses a projected coordinate fallback only in the
exactly comoving limit.

## 5. Multi-step event-location BPTT

### Objective and smoothing

For a trajectory of horizon \(H\), the objective combines terminal and
trajectory error:

\[
\mathcal L_H
=
\frac12(z_H-z_\star)^2
+\frac{0.02}{H}\sum_{n=1}^H(z_n-z_\star)^2.
\]

The initial condition is Gaussian-smoothed around the negative parity edge
\(c=-0.716\overline6\), with standard deviation `0.01`.

Four estimators are compared:

1. **ordinary BPTT**
   \[
   \mathbb E[\mathcal L'_{{\rm branch}}],
   \]
   which differentiates only through the selected event tape;
2. **conservative trace**
   uses the midpoint of the two branch derivatives at each event. For a
   continuous sampling density this modifies only a measure-zero trace and
   therefore does not recover a value jump;
3. **randomized smoothing**
   uses the antithetic Gaussian score estimator
   \[
   \frac{[\mathcal L(c+\sigma\epsilon)
   -\mathcal L(c-\sigma\epsilon)]\epsilon}{2\sigma};
   \]
4. **jump-aware event BPTT**
   adds every detected event-location term
   \[
   \sum_b[\mathcal L]_b\,\rho_\sigma(b-c)
   \]
   to the ordinary pathwise integral.

The reference is a dense Gaussian score integral, which includes both smooth
and distributional contributions.

### Results

| Horizon | Material jumps | Ordinary error | Conservative error | Randomized error | Randomized standard error | Jump-aware error |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | `18.6388` | `18.6388` | `0.130954` | `0.256913` | `4.80783e-3` |
| 4 | 1 | `0.179238` | `0.179238` | `2.29487e-3` | `2.54790e-3` | `4.78908e-5` |
| 8 | 1 | `8.96113e-2` | `8.96113e-2` | `6.24167e-4` | `1.31894e-3` | `2.32541e-5` |
| 16 | 1 | `4.48052e-2` | `4.48052e-2` | `7.30295e-5` | `6.49287e-4` | `1.15907e-5` |

The jump-aware estimator is best at every tested horizon. Randomized
smoothing also captures the missing jump in expectation, but with Monte Carlo
uncertainty. Ordinary and conservative branch traces agree because neither
contains the value-jump impulse.

This comparison makes an important distinction: a Clarke or conservative
branch derivative is useful for a locally Lipschitz continuous switch, but it
cannot replace an event-location term when the objective itself jumps.

## Limitations and next scientific work

1. The independent EOS/transport table is generated from a fixed
   relaxation-time kinetic benchmark. It is not experimental data and does
   not include quantum statistics, reactions, electromagnetic transport, or
   a calibrated neutron-star EOS.
2. The mobility matrix's worst relative cross-coefficient error is about 38%.
   A wider context network or direct Cholesky-target loss may reduce that
   without compromising PSD structure.
3. The phase threshold is an effective contextual classifier. Its bootstrap
   interval measures trajectory sampling uncertainty under the generator, not
   model-form uncertainty or real-world uncertainty.
4. The event-aware experiment centers on one parity boundary. More centers,
   simultaneous KKT/phase events, learned parameter perturbations, and
   event-time derivatives with respect to network weights should follow.
5. The final characteristic margin is intentionally close to its `0.01`
   reserve. Any change to the master, data normalization, or phase edge must
   rerun continuation rather than inherit this scale.
6. The current 128-step evidence is dense and reproducible but empirical; it
   is not a global hyperbolicity or stability proof.
7. The Vulkan module freezes inference. Training and uncertainty estimation
   remain CPU-side, which is the intended qualification boundary.

Within those limits, the requested boundary has been crossed honestly: the
system now learns from an independent constitutive generator, quantifies
phase uncertainty, produces covariant PSD transport, accounts for
event-location derivatives through time, earns additional Carter authority
through explicit margins, replays without training, and exports only the
qualified frozen result to the shader backend.
